The problem, in one paragraph

When a work finishes above its administrative sanction, the exchequer pays the difference and the public loses the benefit of the delay. Audit reports of the Comptroller and Auditor General of India have repeatedly recorded large numbers of central-scheme works completing above sanctioned cost, with percentage cost escalation concentrated in small rural works and in department-executed packages. A prediction model cannot stop an overrun, but it can rank projects by risk before sanction — telling an engineer where to insist on a detailed survey, a geotechnical report and a realistic schedule.

PrecisionOf what we flagged, how many truly overran

            How the two models decide

            Drag the test project and watch which training projects become its neighbours, then how the vote changes with K.

            KNN, live

            —

            Vertical axis is not a rupee figure: it is a scaled coordinate standing for "how many category fields differ", so Euclidean distance here means exactly what it means in the model.

            Euclidean distance, as the model computes it

            d(q, p) = √ Σᵢ (qᵢ − pᵢ)²  over the one-hot and scaled columns

            Two projects that differ in n one-hot fields land at distance √n, because 1 − 0 = 1. The two numeric fields contribute their standardised difference, so a ₹900 crore Smart City scheme is not automatically "far" from a ₹40 lakh rural road.

            K-Nearest Neighbours

            1. Encode the query project exactly like the training rows.
            2. Measure its distance to all 560 training projects.
            3. Keep the K closest.
            4. Count the votes: majority class wins.
            5. Report the winning fraction as the risk percentage.

            Nothing is learned in the usual sense — the training set is the model, which is why it is so easy to explain and so slow on millions of rows.

            Random Forest

            1. Build 300 decision trees, each on a bootstrap sample of rows.
            2. At every split pick the field and cut that most reduces impurity (Gini).
            3. Average the trees' probabilities — here, the fraction voting "over budget".
            4. Class weights rebalance for the fact that overruns are the minority.

            It wins on this dataset because it can split on rupees and months directly, and because 300 votes are steadier than K neighbours' bare majority.

            Documentation

            About this project

            Method, findings, limitations and what would come next — the parts an examiner reads first.

            Methodology

            Seven fields describe each work: scheme, state, district, execution type, sanctioned rupee amount, scheduled months, and the target — did the actual cost exceed sanction. Categorical fields go through a one-hot encoder configured to ignore unknown categories; the two numeric fields are standardised, because a rupee column spanning three orders of magnitude would otherwise dominate every distance calculation. A stratified 80/20 split with seed 42 keeps 42 % overruns in both halves. KNN and Random Forest are fitted on that identical split and compared on the F1 of the over-budget class, not on accuracy, which the majority class inflates.

            Findings

            • Execution type dominates. Department or PSU-executed works carry the single largest share of the forest's importance — the land, utility and clearance delays stay with the department itself.
            • Geography is second. State matters at this sample size; district, with 59 categories and roughly a dozen rows each, mostly adds noise.
            • Small works slip by a larger percentage. Mobilisation and survey costs are nearly fixed, so they bite hardest on a small sanction.
            • Longer schedules overrun more. Every extra year of scheduled time is more change orders.
            • Water and rural-road schemes lead in overrun rate; Smart City works, being larger and consultant-managed, are lower.

            Limitations

            • The dataset is simulated, so absolute numbers are illustrative — the relationships are what the model genuinely learns.
            • Only six input fields. Land records, seasonal monsoon windows, agency capacity, steel and cement price indices and prior delay history all matter and are absent.
            • Predicted spend uses a stated risk-weighted heuristic, not a trained cost-regression model.
            • Probabilities are the models' own votes, not calibrated, so 58 % should not be read as 58 long-run successes.
            • 140 test rows means one row is 0.7 % of accuracy — figures will move between seeds.

            Future scope

            • Replace the simulated rows with e-Procure / CAG audit extracts and retrain without touching the code — the storage adapter already supports a URL or a bucket.
            • Add SMOTE or class weighting tuned per scheme to lift over-budget recall further.
            • Compare a gradient-boosted model; XGBoost was deliberately skipped here because its compiled binary trips Windows Smart App Control on this machine.
            • Train a second model to predict the overrun amount directly, replacing the heuristic.
            • Calibrate probabilities and report a reliability diagram.
            • Move the chunked pipeline to Spark and the CSV feature store to a Delta or Iceberg table; SPEC_INDIA.md section 8 lists the exact one-line changes.
